**Content SEO** is planning and writing content that satisfies search intent better than competing pages.

### How it works

Search intent is the job behind the query: learn, compare, buy, navigate, troubleshoot, or evaluate. Content SEO starts by matching the format and depth to that intent, then covering the topic with enough specificity for the reader to stop searching.

Good content uses clear examples, original judgment, helpful structure, and links to adjacent ideas. In a knowledge base, [[internal-linking|internal linking]] and topic clusters help show relationships between beginner definitions and deeper notes.

### When to use

Use content SEO when deciding what pages to create, how deep they should be, and how they should connect. It is especially useful for blogs, docs, glossaries, product education, and comparison pages.

### Trade-offs

Broad content can capture more queries but often becomes shallow. Narrow content can be more useful and easier to rank for, but it needs enough related pages around it to build topical coverage.

### Pitfalls

Do not publish pages just because a keyword exists. Thin pages, copied summaries, outdated information, and generic AI-like prose usually fail because they do not add enough value beyond existing results.
