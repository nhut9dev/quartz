**Design critique** is a structured review of how well a design supports its goal.

### How it works

A useful critique starts with the intent: user, task, constraint, and success criteria. Feedback then targets the design decision, not the designer, and connects observations to outcomes.

Good critique separates taste from function. "This feels off" is a starting signal; the useful version names the issue, such as weak [[visual-hierarchy|visual hierarchy]], poor [[../accessibility/color-contrast|color contrast]], unclear [[alignment|alignment]], or spacing that hides relationships.

### When to use

Use critique before implementation, before shipping, and when a screen feels wrong but the problem is not obvious. It is especially useful for [[../product-engineering/|product engineering]] work where design decisions affect workflow speed and error rate.

### Trade-offs

Critique improves quality, but it can slow delivery if it becomes subjective or unbounded. Keep it tied to the task, the audience, and the most important risks.

### Pitfalls

Do not review everything at once. Start with whether the screen supports the task, then move to hierarchy, layout, accessibility, copy, and polish.
