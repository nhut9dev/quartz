**Fundraising** is raising capital to extend your [[runway|runway]], usually by selling **equity** — a share of ownership in the company — to investors.

It happens in staged rounds: pre-seed and seed fund early experiments, then Series A, B, C and beyond fund scaling once you have signs of [[product-market-fit|product-market fit]]. Each round typically buys 12–24 months of runway.

A round is set by a **valuation** (what the company is worth) and the amount raised. Raise $2M at a $8M pre-money valuation and investors own 20% of the resulting $10M company.

Every round causes **dilution**: founders own a smaller percentage afterward. That's fine if the company grows — a smaller slice of a much bigger pie is still a bigger slice.

Investors care about defensibility and growth, so a strong [[moat|moat]] and healthy [[unit-economics|unit economics]] make a round easier to close and the valuation higher.
