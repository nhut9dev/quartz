title: Unit economics

**Unit economics** are the direct revenues and costs tied to a single customer or unit. They reveal whether the business makes or loses money each time it serves someone — before fixed overhead.

The key figure is **contribution margin**: revenue per unit minus the variable costs to deliver it. A positive margin means every sale helps pay for the rest of the company; a negative one means you lose money by growing.

For a subscription business, the math combines lifetime value, [[customer-acquisition-cost|acquisition cost]], and churn. Healthy unit economics are the difference between scaling toward profit and scaling toward collapse.

Strong unit economics depend on [[pricing-strategy|pricing]] (more revenue per unit) and on efficient delivery (lower variable cost per unit).

Investors scrutinize unit economics closely in [[fundraising|fundraising]], because a business that's unprofitable at the unit level rarely fixes itself with scale.
