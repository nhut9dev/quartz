**Recurring revenue** is income that repeats on a predictable schedule, usually from subscriptions. Its predictability is why investors value subscription businesses far more highly than one-off sales.

It's tracked as **MRR** (monthly recurring revenue) and **ARR** (annual recurring revenue, roughly MRR × 12). These run-rate figures show the steady income you can expect if nothing changes.

**Churn** is the share of customers or revenue you lose each period. Churn quietly drains MRR, so even fast sign-ups can't grow the business if the bucket leaks faster than you fill it.

**Net revenue retention** (NRR) measures revenue from existing customers over time, including upgrades and downgrades. NRR above 100% means expansion outpaces churn and you'd grow even with zero new customers.

Recurring revenue depends on retention — the same force behind [[product-market-fit|product-market fit]] — and feeds directly into [[unit-economics|unit economics]] and lifetime value versus [[customer-acquisition-cost|acquisition cost]].
