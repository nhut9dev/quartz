A **moat** is a durable competitive advantage that protects a business from competitors. The term, borrowed from castles, asks: once you succeed, what stops others from copying you and taking your customers?

Common moats include network effects (each user makes the product more valuable), economies of scale, high switching costs, proprietary technology, and a trusted brand.

The strongest moats compound over time. Network effects and data advantages widen as you grow, making the leader harder to catch the bigger it gets.

A moat is different from a head start. Being first or having a clever feature is rarely defensible; a real moat survives well-funded rivals trying to copy you.

Moats raise valuations in [[fundraising|fundraising]] and protect your [[pricing-strategy|pricing]] power — without one, competition erodes margins back toward zero even after you reach [[product-market-fit|product-market fit]].
