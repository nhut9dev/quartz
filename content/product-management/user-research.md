**User research** is the practice of studying real users to understand their needs, behaviors, and pain points. It grounds product decisions in evidence rather than assumptions.

*Qualitative* methods like interviews and usability tests reveal *why* users behave as they do, surfacing motivations and frustrations in their own words. They go deep on a few users.

*Quantitative* methods like surveys and analytics reveal *how many* and *how much*, giving statistically meaningful patterns across many users. They go wide but explain less.

The two are complementary: qualitative research generates hypotheses, quantitative research tests how widespread they are. Strong teams loop between them continuously.

User research is the engine of [[product-discovery|product discovery]] and feeds [[jobs-to-be-done|jobs to be done]] thinking, the [[prd|PRD]], and well-formed [[../scrum/user-story|user stories]].
