title: Customer acquisition cost
description: The average cost to win one new customer, and how it compares to their value.

**Customer acquisition cost** (CAC) is the average cost to win one new customer. You calculate it by dividing total sales and marketing spend over a period by the number of customers gained.

CAC only makes sense next to lifetime value (LTV) — the total profit a customer brings over their relationship. A common rule of thumb is an LTV:CAC ratio of at least 3:1.

The **payback period** is how many months of revenue it takes to earn CAC back. Shorter payback frees up cash faster and keeps your [[runway|runway]] longer; under 12 months is a healthy target.

CAC sits at the center of [[unit-economics|unit economics]] and is shaped by your [[go-to-market|go-to-market]] channels — cheap, organic channels lower it, while paid ads tend to raise it as you scale.

Watch CAC over time: rising CAC with flat LTV is an early warning that growth is getting more expensive than it's worth.
