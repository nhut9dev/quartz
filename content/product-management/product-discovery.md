**Product discovery** is the work of figuring out *what* to build and whether it's worth building, before committing engineering effort. Its goal is to reduce risk early, when changes are cheap.

Discovery answers four questions: will users want it (value), can they use it (usability), can we build it (feasibility), and does it work for the business (viability). Skipping discovery often leads to shipping features nobody needs.

Common discovery methods include [[user-research|user research]], prototyping, and small experiments. The point is to test the riskiest assumptions fast, not to gather perfect certainty.

Discovery pairs with delivery: discovery decides what's worth building, delivery builds it well. The two run continuously and in parallel, not as sequential phases.

Discovery findings shape the [[prd|PRD]], the [[product-roadmap|roadmap]], and ultimately the search for [[../startup/product-market-fit|product-market fit]].
