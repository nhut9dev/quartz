A **prioritization framework** is a structured way to decide what to build next when you have more ideas than capacity. It replaces gut feeling and loudest-voice-wins with a repeatable, defensible process.

**RICE** scores each item by Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort, ranking by `(Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort`. It works well for comparing many independent ideas.

**MoSCoW** sorts work into Must-have, Should-have, Could-have, and Won't-have. It's simple and great for scoping a release or aligning stakeholders quickly.

**Kano** classifies features by how they affect satisfaction: basic expectations, performance needs, and delighters. It helps you balance must-haves against differentiators.

Frameworks inform, not dictate. Feed their output into the [[product-roadmap|roadmap]] and the [[../scrum/product-backlog|Product Backlog]], and revisit priorities as new [[product-metrics|metrics]] arrive.
