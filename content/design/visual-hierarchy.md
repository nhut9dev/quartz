**Visual hierarchy** is the ordering of information by visual importance.

### How it works

Hierarchy is created through size, weight, contrast, position, spacing, color, depth, and repetition. A user should be able to glance at a screen and understand what is primary, what is secondary, and what can wait.

Strong hierarchy usually starts with content priority. The visual treatment should reflect the task: a destructive action, a chart outlier, a form error, and a page title need different signals.

### When to use

Use hierarchy whenever a screen contains competing information: dashboards, forms, settings, tables, cards, landing pages, and navigation. It should connect to [[typography|typography]], [[color-theory|color theory]], and [[spacing|spacing]].

### Trade-offs

High contrast hierarchy speeds scanning but can make secondary information feel hidden. Flat hierarchy feels calm but forces the user to read too much before acting.

### Pitfalls

If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized. Too many bold labels, accent colors, shadows, or large headings create noise instead of priority.
