A **sorting algorithm** rearranges data into a chosen order, such as ascending numbers or alphabetical strings.

Common sorting algorithms include bubble sort, insertion sort, merge sort, quicksort, and heapsort. Practical language runtimes usually provide a built-in sort, but understanding sorting helps explain tradeoffs around time, memory, stability, and comparison functions.

Efficient general-purpose sorting is often `O(n log n)`. Simple nested-loop sorts are usually `O(n^2)`.
