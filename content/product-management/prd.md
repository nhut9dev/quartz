A **Product Requirements Document** (PRD) describes what a product or feature should do, who it serves, and why it matters. It aligns engineering, design, and stakeholders on a shared understanding before work begins.

A good PRD captures the problem, target users, goals, success metrics, scope, and key requirements. It usually links out to [[../scrum/user-story|user stories]] and [[../scrum/acceptance-criteria|acceptance criteria]] rather than restating them.

PRDs range from lightweight to heavyweight. A lightweight PRD is a one-pager focused on the problem and outcome, ideal for fast-moving teams; a heavyweight PRD details flows, edge cases, and constraints for complex or regulated work.

The PRD is typically owned by the [[../scrum/product-owner|Product Owner]] or product manager, and it feeds the [[../scrum/product-backlog|Product Backlog]]. It should describe outcomes, not implementation details.

Treat a PRD as a living document. Validate its assumptions through [[product-discovery|product discovery]] and update it as you learn.
